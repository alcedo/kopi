# Technology Research Source Policy

## Source order

1. Official documentation, release notes, changelogs, repositories, specifications, security advisories, and migration guides.
2. Original research papers, benchmark repositories, and maintainers' technical explanations.
3. Reputable independent analysis with disclosed method and date.
4. Community discussions for adoption experience and leads, never as sole proof of product behavior.

For technical claims, prefer primary sources. Use secondary sources to identify disagreement, operational experience, or evidence the vendor does not publish.

## Freshness

- Record publication or release date and retrieval date.
- Compare the source date with the user's pinned version and decision horizon.
- An undated page is not proof of recency.
- Distinguish the date an article was updated from the date the underlying feature shipped.
- Re-check links used in a recurring radar rather than carrying old summaries forward.

## Claim discipline

- Direct: explicitly stated by an authoritative source.
- Supported: several sources align, but none states the full claim alone.
- Inferred: evidence supports a reasoning chain that must be shown.
- Speculative: plausible but weakly supported.
- Unknown: the searched record does not answer.

Surface source conflicts. Do not average incompatible benchmark methods or compare versions under different workloads as if they were one measurement.
