# Presentation Rubric

Judge the rendered deck, not the source code or generation log.

## Decision quality

- The audience and purpose are apparent in the opening.
- The recommendation, request, or intended takeaway is explicit.
- Alternatives and tradeoffs are fair when the decision requires them.
- The slide sequence builds one coherent argument.

## Evidence

- Material claims have resolvable sources.
- Facts, estimates, assumptions, and recommendations are distinguishable.
- Quantitative statements preserve units, populations, and time windows.
- Unknowns are visible rather than presented as certainty.

## Visual communication

- Each slide has one message expressed by its title.
- Charts use honest scales, labels, comparisons, and denominators.
- Diagrams clarify relationships or sequence.
- Text is legible in a presentation setting.
- No content is clipped, crowded, overlapping, or misaligned.
- Contrast and color use remain understandable without relying on color alone.

## Delivery

- Speaker notes add context instead of repeating the slide.
- Source footnotes are readable and unobtrusive.
- Terminology, numbers, dates, and visual conventions stay consistent.
- The final slide makes the decision or next action unambiguous.

## Verdict

Return `PASS`, `REVISE`, or `BLOCKED`. A deck with unresolved visual defects, unsupported claims, or no clear outcome cannot pass.
