# Performance Review Evidence Synthesis

## Component contract

**Inputs:** Role, supplied feedback, self-reflection, optional goals and period; later, manager observations.

**Output:** A confidential source ledger and theme evidence map, including contradictions, limitations, and material questions. It supplies grounded ideas to writing workflows; it does not decide ratings or write the employee report.

Use the shared [evidence fields](../../kopi-mode/references/artifact-model.md#evidence). Add local source IDs, reviewer identity or supplied anonymous label, source kind (reviewer, self, manager), locator, and observation/event where useful. A locator can be a file and heading or a pasted-message label and paragraph. Never invent dates, identities, or missing metadata. Direct evidence of what a reviewer wrote is not automatically proof of the underlying claim.

## Read and reconcile

1. Inventory every supplied source. Briefly summarize each reviewer's strengths and improvement observations; distinguish no comment on a theme from disagreement. Include self-reflection separately and identify inaccessible inputs.
2. Extract behavior, context, impact, and limits faithfully. Preserve short exact excerpts privately when useful, distinguishing quotations from summaries. Keep claimed metrics separate from verified calculations and identify shared/team contributions without assigning sole credit.
3. Group observations into themes without losing the source pointers. For each theme, distinguish separate firsthand examples, one event described by several people, secondhand repetition, and unsupported opinion. Count reviewers and events separately if counts help; do not turn mentions into scores.
4. Compare self-reflection with reviewer evidence: aligned, partially supported, uncorroborated, or in tension. Stated focus, intent, observed behavior, and demonstrated impact are different claims. Self-reflection is not a record of formally agreed goals.
5. Preserve counterevidence and context differences. A positive experience on one project does not erase a negative one elsewhere. One well-supported significant observation can matter more than many vague comments. Reviewers' access to the work matters more than their seniority or forceful wording.

For each proposed strength or improvement theme, include a private table:

| Source / locator | What the person said | Example and impact | Relationship to other evidence / limits |
|---|---|---|---|

Include all relevant sources for that theme; use the source inventory to account for reviewers who did not address it. This is a schema, not a table to return empty. Separate later manager observations into their own rows. Do not silently rewrite the original reviewer record when the manager adds context or disagrees.

## Calibrate the assessment

Use recurrence language only when supported. Prefer a scoped statement such as “feedback describes late updates on one release” over “consistently communicates poorly.” Do not force equal numbers of strengths and improvements or treat silence as agreement. Vague labels such as “executive presence” are not established behavioral deficits; record the opinion and missing example privately rather than inventing a behavior to explain it.

Use qualified wording for an ordinary evidence gap and record what could strengthen the assessment. An unverified percentage can be omitted while recognizing a supported contribution. Ask only if the gap could change the overall conclusion, reverse a consequential theme, or turn an uncertain serious allegation into a finding. State which conclusion depends on the answer, keep it unresolved, and continue unrelated synthesis. Do not require a missing formal goal or an example for every comment before proceeding.

Proposed actions and manager interpretations are not historical evidence. If the manager selects a theme with conflicting support, retain the disagreement and match the wording to the evidence. Keep unsupported or excluded ideas visible in private notes with their disposition.
