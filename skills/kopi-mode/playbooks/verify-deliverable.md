# Verify the Deliverable

**Invoked before any playbook declares done.** It owns the check on the real artifact rather than on the process that produced it.

1. Open what the user will actually consume. Render the deck. Read back the created event. Recompute the numbers. Re-query the updated record. Resolve every source link. A successful command, a completed step, and a worker's summary are not the check.
2. Check the artifact against the frame first: the named consumer, the decision, the requested format, the requested scope, and the stated limits.
3. Check the evidence layer. Every load-bearing claim resolves to its source. Dates and as-of stamps are present. Confidence and freshness labels match what the source actually supports.
4. Check the failure modes of this artifact's own kind. Overflow, contrast, and chart honesty on a rendered deck. Time zone, participants, and duplicates on an event. Population, units, and selection effects on an analysis. Owners, staleness, and duplicate identities on a register. Audience boundaries on a confidential report.
5. Fix the artifact and check it again. A note describing the defect is not a fix. Re-run the check on the corrected artifact, not on the change you made to it.
6. Report what you verified and how, plus what you could not verify and why. An unverifiable claim is stated as unverified. It is not dropped and not softened.

This playbook is the floor. A finding that needs an adversarial pass routes to [critical review](critical-review.md).
