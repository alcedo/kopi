# Compare Options

**Invoked before any consequential or hard-to-reverse choice.** It owns the option set and the comparison basis, not the subject matter of the decision.

1. Write the choice as a question with its constraints, its decision horizon, and who owns the answer. A cheap and reversible choice does not need this playbook. Pick and move on.
2. Classify the fork before generating anything. When observation could settle it, run [sketch options](sketch-options.md) and let the result decide. Reserve the comparison for a choice that only evidence and judgment can settle.
3. Generate at least two structurally different options. Variations of one parameter of one design are not alternatives. Include defer or do nothing whenever it is a real option.
4. Fix the basis before comparing. Same criteria, same evidence standard, same cost horizon for every option. Name the criteria that would change the answer if they moved.
5. Compare on one table. Per option: what it assumes, what it costs, what it forecloses, how it fails, and how reversible it is. Keep the underlying facts identical across rows.
6. Recommend one option with its tradeoffs, the risks that stay unresolved, and the conditions under which the recommendation would change.
7. Route the selection. Present the table and let the user choose when the fork is a product, audience, or preference call. Choose and finish only where that was explicitly delegated. Record what was selected, combined, qualified, deferred, and rejected.

For a choice between framings of the same content rather than between designs, use [compare writing ideas](../references/compare-writing-ideas.md).
